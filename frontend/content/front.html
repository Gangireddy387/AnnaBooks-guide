AnnaBooks feature guide: how the accounting app works

For someone new to the application

AnnaBooks is where a company keeps its books. You create a Client account, open one company, and post invoices, bills, pay, and bank activity into that company. The same numbers then appear on profit and on the balance sheet.

You own the booksEach company is its own set of books. A second company does not inherit the first company’s invoices or bank.
Save before it countsNothing reaches the reports until it is saved. See the one rule below.
Payroll is a recordAnnaBooks records what you pay. It does not file tax, and it does not pay a bill or post an invoice for you.

Start your AnnaBooks subscription

A subscription starts when you create a Client account. That account is yours. You then create the company whose books you will keep.

  1. On the AnnaBooks start page, choose Create account.
  2. Sign in as a Client. The Vendor door is only for a supplier you invite later.
  3. Create one company. Choose the ITConsulting industry if you bill people by the hour; it is the industry that shows the Consultant menu.
  4. Post into that company only. Confirm the company name before you save a payment.

The one rule

Saving an invoice, a bill, a received payment, a bill payment, a deposit, a completed payroll run, or a journal entry writes the books. A draft, a captured email, or a vendor portal bill writes nothing until you open it on the bill screen and click Save and Close; a staged import writes nothing until you apply it. Until then it stays off profit, the balance sheet, and the aging reports. A few screens do not save yet in the current version; their flowcharts end in a dashed "Not posted yet" box.
